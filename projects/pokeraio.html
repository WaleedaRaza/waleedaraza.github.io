THE WRITE-UP

My friends and I wanted a poker platform we trusted without paying just to play amongst ourselves. A lot of the alternatives felt like they were programmed with tools made before 2005, so I started building one.

A weekend project became nine months of enthusiastic hell.

The game was harder than the platform

The surrounding features were substantial: friends, hand histories, rankings, rewards, bot play, and AI analysis of played or imported hands. But the actual game algorithm and turn logic were harder than the social features or the platform around them.

A poker application needs more than an interface that looks like poker. It needs the game to keep making sense as people act. That was where much of the work went, and where my initial estimate of the project stopped being useful.

I also built shuffle-verification features. A deeper technical write-up should explain exactly what they verify and how, rather than treat the phrase as a guarantee about the whole system.

What happened when people used it

My friends and I played extensively. A small beta with a poker club at another university produced feedback about performance and additional social features, including friend systems and leagues.

I haven't given that feedback or distribution the sustained attention they need. I tried promoting the project on a subreddit and got banned. No one read my tweet. Those attempts were experiences, not a distribution strategy.

The public-facing shell has been available, but I'm not presenting the game as an open public launch. There's work to do before that would be an honest description.

PokerAIO is a substantial build and an unfinished product. Both matter. It showed me how much I could make by staying with a difficult problem, and how much work remains after making the software.
