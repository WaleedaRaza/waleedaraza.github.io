THE WRITE-UP

I take notes throughout the day: thoughts, reactions, sources, project ideas, and things I want to return to. Over time, that became years of material spread across different interests.

I used Obsidian, then began opening Cursor and Claude Code in my markdown directories. I spent months working through how to organize notes that ranged from a single line to thousands. Eventually I started writing instructions for AI inside the notes themselves: organize this material, distill these ideas, connect these things.

That led me into agents, then into building an environment around the process.

How it works

Warb gives notes domains and types. A domain describes an area of life or work; a type describes the kind of material inside it. A source, a reaction, a plan, and a principle don't all ask to be treated the same way.

Domains also have charters that describe their purpose and what growth means there. Keeping up an obligation is different from exploring a craft or accumulating knowledge. I want the structure to preserve those differences, rather than flatten everything into documents for a chatbot to search.

The AI companion can retrieve relevant notes, work with their context, and help preserve useful results. Configurable workflows, reports, and integrations extend that beyond an isolated conversation. The aim is for the information I keep to become more usable over time.

How I use it every day

  • Write journal entries
  • Learn about philosophy
  • Log what I like about music
  • Track projects and how they grow
  • Keep what I learn for work and school
  • Get daily reports and suggestions for new content
  • Run large executions across everything I've logged

Where it's going

Warb is live, and I use it every day. My next challenge is making its value understandable to people who haven't spent months building it with me.

Longer term, I want users to share templates ranging from individual note structures to domains and agent workflows. I think a useful library and community around those structures could become more durable than the implementation alone. That's a hypothesis, not a moat I've already built.

It's the best thing I've programmed so far. I still have trouble describing it without showing it.
